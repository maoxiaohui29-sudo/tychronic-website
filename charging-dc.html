DC fast charging, 60 to 720 kW: verified specs

Two ways to build a DC site. All-in-one units put the rectifiers and the connector in one cabinet, from 60 kW to 320 kW. Split systems use a power cabinet of 360, 480 or 720 kW and share that power across up to 12 dispensers. Below are the published figures for both, with the source named. All-in-one or split: what actually differs

An all-in-one charger carries its own power modules. Its output per bay is fixed at the nameplate rating, it needs one grid connection and one foundation, and it can be commissioned bay by bay. That is the cheapest path to a first working site.

A split system separates rectification from the connector. The power cabinet sits at the edge of the site; dispensers stand at the bays. Because the cabinet pools its modules, a 720 kW cabinet can give one vehicle 400 kW while three others take 100 kW each, then redistribute when they leave. You buy flexibility in exchange for DC cabling between cabinet and dispensers.

Matching power to the site

Charger nameplate is not what the site draws. What the site draws is set by the vehicles, and the connection has to cover the worst 15 minutes, not the average. Three numbers decide the specification:

  • Connection capacity. A 720 kW cabinet needs a 400 V feed of 1100 A. Where the transformer cannot carry that, a cabinet plus a 261 kWh buffer often fits where a bigger connection does not.
  • Simultaneity. Twelve bays at 60 kW each is 720 kW only if all twelve charge at once. Public hubs typically see 30 to 50 percent simultaneous demand, which is why pooling beats fixed power per bay.
  • Vehicle mix. A 500 A connector only pays off if the vehicles can accept it. Mixed fleets usually want a few high-power bays and more medium-power bays, not uniform 320 kW everywhere.

DC fast charging questions, answered

What is a DC fast charger?

It converts three-phase AC to DC and feeds the battery directly, bypassing the vehicle's onboard charger. The range here runs from 60 kW all-in-one units to 720 kW cabinets shared across up to 12 connectors, at 150–1000 Vdc.

All-in-one or split system?

All-in-one (Jupiter V4, Titan V4, Titan V5) fixes power per bay and is fastest to install. Split (Nova cabinet plus dispensers) pools power across bays and suits hubs with four or more bays and variable demand.

What power range is available?

All-in-one: 60, 80, 120, 180, 240 and 320 kW. Cabinets: 360 kW (Nova V2), 480 kW (Nova V5), 720 kW (Nova V4). Dispensers draw from the cabinet.

Which connectors?

CCS2 standard, as CCS2+CCS2 or CCS2+GB/T. GB/T-only variants exist for that market. NACS is quoted per project and is not in the published datasheets.

Is an 800 V EV supported?

Yes — 150–1000 Vdc on Jupiter V4, Titan V4, Titan V5 and all Nova cabinets. GB/T outputs are rated 150–950 Vdc.

Maximum current per connector?

Titan V4: 300 A rated, 500 A max. Neptune: 500 A max, 400 A per connector when two charge at once. Titan V5: 300 A on CCS2.

Which standards and certifications?

Built to IEC 61851-1, -21-2 and -23, with LVD and RED compliance. Per model: CE-RED and CB (Jupiter V4, Nova), CE, CB and Eichrecht (Titan V4), CE, CB and RCM (Titan V5).

Which backend protocols?

OCPP 1.6J throughout. OCPP 2.0.1 on Titan V5 and Nova V5; Titan V4 is documented as 1.6J with a stated upgrade path. Vehicle side: DIN 70121 and ISO 15118.

How many bays per cabinet?

Up to 12 connectors on Nova V2, V4 and V5. Nova V2 comes in two layouts: 180 kW or 360 kW maximum per connector.

Operating conditions and IP rating?

IP55, IK10 (IK08 screen), rated to 2000 m. -30 to +50 deg C, or -30 to +55 on Titan V5; derating above 50 deg C.

How long are the cables?

5 m on Jupiter V4 and Titan units, 6 m optional on Titan V4 and V5. Triton uses 10 m. Cable management systems extend usable reach.

Can DC charging be combined with battery storage?

Yes, and it is the usual fix where grid capacity caps charger power. See the 261 kWh cabinet page for verified storage figures.

Price and lead time?

No public price list. Quotes are itemised after site requirements are received; delivery schedules are confirmed per project.
